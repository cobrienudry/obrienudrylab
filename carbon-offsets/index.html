Carbon offsets in corporate reporting

We are tracing how large U.S. companies talk about carbon offsets (credits, removals, renewable energy certificates, "carbon neutral" claims) in their sustainability reports and shareholder documents, and how that language changed over the last decade. Pick one of two tasks.

Task 1: Collect reports

The spreadsheet lists the Fortune 500 (by rank) with links to sustainability reports for 2015–2025, mostly found automatically. Many links are unchecked, some years are missing, and almost none of the annual shareholder documents are there yet. Your job is to make one company's row set complete and correct, and save the PDFs.

Open the spreadsheet ↗

Steps

  1. Claim a company. Find a company where the coder column is blank on all its rows. Type your name in coder on every row for that company. Work down the list in rank order unless you've been given a specific company.
  2. Check every link that's already there. Open each url. Confirm it opens the right company's report for the year in report_year. If the link is dead or wrong, find the correct one (start from source_page_url) and replace it. Write what you did in notes (e.g. link checked, replaced dead link, year corrected from 2021 to 2022).
  3. Fill missing sustainability-report years, 2015–2025. Look on the company's sustainability or investor-relations site, then responsibilityreports.com, then the Wayback Machine. Add a new row for each report you find. If a year doesn't exist, add one row with the year, a blank url and a note saying where you looked.
  4. Add the annual shareholder documents, 2015–2025. For each year add a row for the annual report to shareholders (the glossy report with the CEO letter; if the company only files a 10-K "wrap", use that). Find these on the investor-relations site under "Annual reports" or "SEC filings", or on SEC EDGAR (the 10-K). Put the document type at the start of report_title, as in the table below.
  5. Download each PDF and save it to the shared folder your supervisor gave you, named RANK_Company_YEAR_TYPE.pdf, e.g. 001_Amazon_2023_SR.pdf or 021_Chevron_2019_AR.pdf. Rank is three digits, the company name has no spaces.
  6. Finish the company. When every year has a row for each document type, add done after your name in coder on the first row (e.g. Maya – done) and claim the next company.

Task 2: Write a summary

Choose one company with at least five years of reports in the spreadsheet. Read how it discusses carbon offsets in its earliest, middle and latest reports, and write a short summary (about 400–600 words) of what changed.

Open the spreadsheet ↗

How to read the reports

  1. Claim the company: type your name – summary in the coder column of its first row, so no one else picks it.
  2. Read at least three reports spread across time (e.g. 2016, 2020, 2024). Search each PDF (Ctrl/⌘ F) for:
    offsetcarbon creditcarbon neutralnet zeronet-zeroremovalRECrenewable energy certificatecompensatavoided emissionsnature-basedVerraGold StandardSBTiinsettingbeyond value chain
  3. For each report, note the page numbers where offsets come up. Every claim in your summary needs a year and page.

What to look for

  • Presence: When do offsets first appear? Do they ever disappear?
  • Role in targets: Are offsets counted toward a carbon-neutral or net-zero goal, or reported separately ("beyond value chain")? Did that change?
  • Type: Avoidance credits (e.g. forest protection, cookstoves) vs removals (reforestation, direct air capture); RECs used to claim zero Scope 2 emissions.
  • Quantity: Tonnes retired or purchased, or money spent, if reported.
  • Quality claims: Named standards or registries (Verra, Gold Standard, ICVCM), named projects, third-party verification.
  • Tone: Promotional, defensive, hedged? Do later reports mention criticism, retire a claim, or shift to "contributions"?
